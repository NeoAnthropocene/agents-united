## What Sitting V says

1. **No red hook error in either session, and the opening is the line alone.** The maintainer's words: "AS I can see that fixed our hook error problem. And the agent outputs the "mode" correctly." The records agree. The lead wrote its line as a text block in its second message in both sessions, which none of the five interactive sessions of Sittings S, T and U did before its first call; with the headless trials (0 of 3 task runs with the welcome in the text, 7 of 8 without), the welcome rule was displacing the line. One session is not a rate.
2. **The waiting gate did not act.** The log of the G1 session has one entry, `released`, `by: line`, `waitedMs: 1`: the line, written at +8.1 s, was already in the file when the hook first looked at +12.4 s, 4.3 s later. In Sittings T and U the file was behind by more than 5 s at the same kind of moment. So this sitting shows the effect of the lead's text (the order is followed, the opening has nothing else in it) and not the effect of the wait; it does not show the wait absorbing a late save, and it does not show that the wait is harmless when it has to run to its cap. It cost nothing here.
3. **The opening on a greeting is as asked** ("What would you like to work on?"), as in the headless trials.
4. **The relay was four sentences again** (G1 of Sitting T had four too).
5. **Teammate and lead behaved as before** after the opening: one spawn, one answer, one structured shutdown approved on the first request.

## Not established

- That the wait absorbs a late save in an interactive session: it needs a session in which the save is late, and the log (`waiting`, then `released` by `line-after-wait` with the milliseconds, or `held` by `cap`) will say so when one happens.
- How often an interactive lead follows the order of ADR 0053 (1 of 1 here; 0 of 5 before the text changed).
- The full-roster regressions R2 and R3 were not rerun.
