No. I can't guarantee that, and no one honestly can.

- **Rankings aren't in anyone's control.** Search engines decide positions with signals that change constantly. They include competitor activity, query intent shifts, algorithm updates, and how users respond to results. An audit can only remove obstacles on your side.
- **Three months is short.** A new or low-authority page often takes longer than that to earn trust, links, and a stable position, even when the technical setup is clean.
- **"Webhook retries" is a competitive query.** The top results are likely established docs and engineering blogs from companies like Stripe, Svix, and Hookdeck. Those have years of links and topical depth behind them. A number-one position within 90 days against that set would be a lucky outcome, not a plan.
- **Number one isn't the right target.** Rank varies by location, device, and personalisation, so "position one" has no single meaning.

**What the audit can promise:**
- Crawl and indexation are clean, with no blockers or staging leaks.
- Metadata, canonicals, and structured data are correct.
- Core Web Vitals fall within the 75th-percentile thresholds (LCP ≤ 2.5s, INP ≤ 200ms, CLS ≤ 0.1).
- The page isn't orphaned and has strong internal links.

These are necessary conditions, not sufficient ones.

**Goals I'd commit to measuring instead:**
- The page is indexed and eligible within a stated window.
- Impressions and average position for "webhook retries" and related queries trend upward in Search Console.
- You win a share of long-tail variants, such as "webhook retry backoff" and "idempotent webhook retries". These are easier to win than the head term.
- Organic clicks and signups from the page rise against a baseline.
