# Copywriting Frameworks

Frameworks (problem-agitate-solve, before-after-bridge, the four-part page story) are scaffolds, not content: copy fails when a scaffold is filled with adjectives instead of facts from the product, the customer and the numbers. The order of work is fixed: source material, voice, one claim, framework, audit.

## Overview & Purpose
For Kaan and Yavuz. It writes words only: layout is Jamileh's, typed section props Deniz's, tracking Jale's, and what the law allows a claim to say Defne's.

## Execution Triggers
Load it before writing any headline, page section, ad, email or post, and when reviewing someone else's copy. Do not use it for UI microcopy inside a product (`ux-writing`) or for long editorial articles (Yavuz works from a content brief and uses this only for the title, intro and call to action).

## Input/Output Requirements
Inputs, asked for and never invented: the audience and its state (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, ready to buy), the offer, three to five true, specific facts (numbers, outcomes, constraints, who uses it), real customer language (tickets, reviews, interview quotes), existing copy for voice, the one action the piece should cause.

Output: the copy; its single claim in one line; the framework and why it fits; the voice profile used; a claim audit table; the **evidence** list, where each fact came from. Typed section props only when the lead asks and Deniz consumes them. Shapes: [examples/templates.md](examples/templates.md).

## Step-by-Step Runbook
1. **Read the source material before you write a word**: product documents, changelog, reviews, the existing page. Collect the phrases customers use for their problem; with none, say so and mark every audience statement an assumption.
2. **Build a short voice profile from real samples** (five to twenty if they exist): sentence length, how direct claims are, use of numbers, what the brand never says. Reuse it across the campaign so the pieces read as one author; with no samples, write plainly and flag the voice as unconfirmed.
3. **Choose one claim per piece**, a sentence a sceptic could check ("Invoices go out in under a minute", not "the fastest invoicing"): a piece that says three things persuades of none.
4. **Pick the framework by reader state**: unaware, lead with a situation they recognise; problem-aware, PAS; solution-aware, contrast and proof; ready to buy, remove doubt; before-after-bridge for case studies and launches. Detail: [references/frameworks.md](references/frameworks.md).
5. **Write specific.** Replace every adjective with a number, a name or a mechanism. One call to action, a verb plus the outcome ("Send your first invoice"), never "Learn more" when the next step is a signup.
6. **Cut the banned phrases** ([references/banned-phrases.md](references/banned-phrases.md)) and restate the fact beneath each: they mark copy with no fact to say.
7. **Audit before hand-off.** Claim audit: every factual statement has a source, or becomes an opinion, or goes. Five-second test on the headline and first screen: who is it for, what does it do, why act now? Cross-channel check: the ad promise matches the page, the subject line matches the body.
8. **Hand off.** Final copy to the requester (Jale for campaign pieces, Jamileh for layout with the hierarchy noted, Deniz as typed props if asked); numerical, comparative, health, finance or earnings claims to Defne before publication.

Anti-patterns, each with its reason:
- Filling a framework with adjectives: the scaffold shows and nothing is said.
- Three claims in one hero section: none of them lands.
- An invented statistic or testimonial to complete the scaffold: one discovery ends the reader's trust.
- Writing before reading the product or a customer's own words: the copy answers a question nobody asked.
- Different voices in the ad, the page and the email: it reads as three companies.
- A closing question used only to provoke replies: it spends goodwill for a metric.

## Edge Cases & Error Recovery
- **No facts to use**: do not write persuasive copy; return the five facts you need and write only a plain description.
- **A fact you cannot source**: remove it, or print it as the client's belief ("the team believes...") and flag it under Open items.
- **Two audiences in one piece**: split the piece; one audience, one claim.
- **Urgency or scarcity that is not real**: refuse the false version, offer a true one (a real date, a real limit) and say why.
- **Voice conflicts with the claim** (a playful brand, a serious number): lead with the number, keep the voice elsewhere.

## Verification Checklist
- [ ] One claim per piece, stated in one checkable sentence.
- [ ] Every number, comparison and testimonial has a source in the evidence list, or is flagged an assumption, or is removed.
- [ ] The five-second test is answered in writing for the headline and first screen.
- [ ] No banned phrase remains; one specific call to action.
- [ ] The voice profile used is named; if samples were missing, that is stated.
- [ ] Hand-offs name Jale, Jamileh, Deniz and Defne with what each receives.
