# Source-preserving edit, mix and master

## Prepare edits before alignment

Record original hashes, selected take, consent/licence references and applicable approved transcript. Included speech requires Kaan-approved exact words and voice consent; wordless cues need no invented narration or Kaan approval. Mark absent speech, transcript/captions, pronunciation and voice-consent records not applicable with a reason; all source rights still apply. Decode and listen before editing: check any missing/clipped words, room changes, obvious distortion and actual source duration. A metadata duration is a lead, not a decoded sample count. Work in new revision paths and keep an edit log linking originals to processed copies.

Trim on natural boundaries while preserving word attacks/breath tails. Use auditioned short fades to avoid hard discontinuities; for two contiguous clips, choose a crossfade shape/overlap by listening, since equal-power fades can raise correlated material and equal-gain fades can dip unrelated material. Repair noise only if speech remains natural; a new pickup is preferable when a missing consonant changes meaning. Resample a production copy to the project rate as needed; it cannot recover lost fidelity.

Loop or edit music deliberately with transitions inside the cue reservation. Duck music around phrases, allowing smooth attack/release and room for consonants; listen to the actual effect instead of assuming one fixed dB reduction suits every track. Print approved fades/crossfades/ducking into a new source or keep editable automation with an explicit bounce. Note whether ducking is included in delivered cue stems. The bundled mixer only trims/resamples/gains/places/pads cue sources, sums them and masters. It does not perform creative repair, music looping, crossfades or automatic ducking.

A song-model output enters as a source. A lyric-to-song or text-to-music render may arrive at 16, 32, 44.1 or 48 kHz as one stereo mix or as separate vocal/instrumental tracks; record the actual codec, rate, channels and hash, then resample a production copy to the project rate as an edit that retains the original. A dual-track or split output becomes an aligned-stem candidate only after the full-duration padded-stem policy is applied, and it is measured like any other cue source; a single mixed song stays one source, not invented stems. Sung words still need Kaan's approved lyrics and a listening review by a competent speaker, and a generated vocal is not a cleared performance.

## Mixer input and timebase

`mix` contains `timelineRevision`, optional `timelineFingerprint`, `tracks`, and optional `targetLufs`/`truePeakDbtp`. With saved preferences, the loudness target must match and a processing peak may be lower than or equal to the approved true-peak ceiling. Each track uses `id`, `kind`, `path`, `startFrame`, `endFrame`, optional `gainDb` (-96..12), optional `trimStartSeconds` (>=0), and nonempty `provenance`. IDs/kinds/ranges exactly match every approved timeline cue once. Supply project-relative local source paths; unsafe/private paths, traversal and resolved escape links are refused.

For a track, source trim starts at `round(trimStartSeconds * 48000)` sample frames; cue length is `round(endFrame*48000/fps) - round(startFrame*48000/fps)`; delay is `round(startFrame*48000/fps)`. Round each timeline endpoint first so adjacent half-open cues meet without overlap or gaps. The whole output is `round(totalFrames*48000/fps)` sample frames. At 60fps a frame is 800 sample frames; `[450,474)` is 0.4 seconds / 19,200 sample frames. At 59fps `[1,2)` spans sample frames `[814,1627)`, exactly 813. Inspect decoded boundaries. A too-short source is padded, not stretched or looped. Detect missing performance/tails before accepting the handoff.

## Preserve mix relationships

Every emitted cue stem is stereo48k PCM24, begins at timeline zero and spans the full approved audio duration, whether film or standalone. The approved source gain and placement remain printed; no independent loudness normalization occurs. The floating PCM pre-master sums them with `amix normalize=0`, preserving the intended summation gain without automatic scaling. With these equal full-duration padded stems, automatic normalization scales the sum globally rather than inherently changing pairwise stem gain ratios.

The master is separately processed using two-pass loudnorm with measured pre-master values, output as stereo48k PCM24 and measured again. Saved project preferences are authoritative: full strict preferences must validate, fps/width/height must match the timeline, and conflicting loudness targets or peaks above the approved ceiling fail. A valid lower `mix.truePeakDbtp` selects stricter processing for all measurement/mastering passes without rewriting preferences, source gain, cue timing or stems. Local loudnorm still requires -9..0 dBTP. With no preferences file, shared defaults are -16 LUFS +/-1 and <=-1 dBTP unless valid standalone overrides are supplied. For standalone sound, Sound supplies the Lead-approved timeline using the same canonical schema. Schema-required video preferences remain unused defaults; matching their timebase fields for the helper does not require a renderer, storyboard or video. Do not delete an existing preferences file to take that fallback.

For AAC delivery, Sound can start with `"truePeakDbtp": -1.5` in the versioned mix while the approved project ceiling remains `-1`. That 0.5 dB processing headroom is below the ceiling, not permission to raise it. Create a fresh Sound package and hand it to Motion through Chris; render and measure the actual final mux against the unchanged project loudness/peak limits. Codec overshoot depends on the signal and encoder, so this starting margin is not a guarantee. A failed mux remains failed until an allowed correction is remeasured; do not merely rerender identical audio or weaken the threshold.

Two-pass processing is an attempt to meet a preset; the final remeasurement decides acceptance. Target/tolerance and true-peak ceiling all matter. A master may include nonlinear processing, so summing cue stems need not reproduce the finished master. Preserve both pre-master and master with the declared stem policy rather than promising a false master reconstruction.

If speech intelligibility requires balance changes, change the appropriate source/automation/gain, then create a new final revision and remeasure. Do not raise every stem to the master target. Listen to transitions in context and to the entire export at comfortable matched levels, including mono and a small speaker. A checksum or LUFS number cannot detect wrong words, poor pronunciation, masking or an unsuitable emotional treatment.
