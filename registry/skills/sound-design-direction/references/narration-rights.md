# Narration, pronunciation and source rights

Kaan owns approved copy whenever copy/speech is included. For wordless cues, speech, transcript, captions, pronunciation and voice-consent records are not applicable with a reason; no narration or Kaan approval is invented. Music/SFX source rights still require review. Preserve exact spelling, claims and brand names in the transcript and canonical VO cue. A delivery markup is a separate file: pauses, emphasis and phonetic hints must not silently replace visible or spoken words. Record script revision, language/locale, target audience, desired register, pace, pronunciation of names/acronyms/numbers and the approver of each exception.

## Before recording or generation

- Specify whether the performer is supplied, newly recorded, a licensed stock voice or an authorized generated voice. Do not infer permission from a publicly available clip.
- Record the consent/release reference, permitted uses, placements/territories, duration, edit/generation permissions and any required disclosure. For cloning or likeness, obtain specific authorization for that use through Chris; ordinary permission to play a recording is insufficient evidence.
- For music and SFX, identify owner/licence, source URL or project-relative receipt, attribution obligations, edit/loop/synchronization permission and intended distribution. "Royalty free" is a label to investigate, not a blanket usage finding. Route unresolved terms to the responsible rights reviewer through Chris.
- Keep consent and private identity documents in the client's controlled location; the production manifest can cite a safe nonsecret evidence reference. Never include credentials or unnecessary personal data in generation prompts or public media packs.

## Scratch and final voice

Read the script naturally and record or obtain authorized scratch VO. Measure the actual duration, phrase boundaries, breathing and pauses; words-per-minute is a planning estimate. A 36-word script in 12 seconds estimates 180 words/minute before pauses, but the performance decides whether it works. For film, reserve visual holds from actual marks; for standalone audio, Sound uses those marks in its Lead-approved cue/timing record. Scratch may use an approved temporary voice, but label it and prevent it from becoming a final deliverable accidentally.

After copy, pronunciation and performance approval, deliver a source-preserving final recording/take ID and exact transcript. Retain natural consonants and breath tails at edits. Do not time-stretch the final performance to a frozen picture unless intelligibility and the change are approved. If it does not fit, send Chris a measured choice: approved recopy with Kaan, a new performance, or revised timing with Motion for film or Sound for standalone audio, approved through Chris.

## Generated singing and music with vocals

Lyrics are copy. When a deliverable includes sung words, Kaan's approved exact lyrics are
the script: preserve spelling, claims, brand names and line order in the transcript and the
canonical cue, and treat a delivery markup (pauses, emphasis, phonetic hints) as a separate
file that never replaces the sung words. A synthetic singing voice is not a person's voice,
but the same no-imitation rule applies: a style prompt must not name or imitate a named
living performer without specific authorization, and a licence for the model weights grants
no lyric rights and no rights in a reference recording. A generated vocal take is a source:
check word coverage and phrasing with a competent speaker of the lyric language, keep the
take identity and model/parameter record, and send an unusable take back through Chris
rather than editing the words to fit it. Wordless instrumental music keeps the speech,
transcript/caption, pronunciation and voice-consent records not applicable with a reason;
its library or model licence review still applies.

## Listening checks that numbers miss

Check names, numbers, negation and qualifiers against approved copy word by word. Listen for clipped word starts, repeated/missing syllables, synthetic mispronunciation, unstable room tone and sibilance. Confirm that music/effects leave key words understandable at low comfortable volume. Review the language with a competent speaker; a model's language label does not establish pronunciation quality. Captions derive from the final approved words and actual timing, including meaningful non-speech cues when requested for accessibility.

Anti-patterns: "make it sound like" a named living performer without suitable authorization; generating first and asking for consent afterwards; deleting a legal qualifier to hit duration; marking a voice approved because synthesis succeeded. Evidence includes the exact approved script, pronunciation decisions, source/take identity, rights references and named listening reviewer, each with an explicit status.
