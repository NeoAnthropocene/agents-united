# Worked example: a 12-second support portal film

Fictional planning example only; no audio, voice permission, client approval or listening result is implied. Chris included sound for a 1920x1080 60fps film, 720 frames. Kaan's example script is exactly: "Requests arrive from everywhere. Give each one a clear owner. Meet Nest." Jamileh's visual direction is quiet and practical. Existing preferences select Native/local, no providers, -16 LUFS +/-1 and true peak <=-1 dBTP.

## Direction and frame proposal

Use a dry conversational recording, sparse soft plucks and one short completion tick. Avoid percussion beneath the first sentence. Music is a connective bed, not a beat grid that forces visual changes. Leave a brief rest before "Meet Nest" and let the music resolve after the final consonant, within the CTA hold.

| Range at 60fps | Proposed event | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [0,240) | Scene: requests arrive; VO [24,204), exact first sentence | An opening 0.4 seconds allows visual orientation |
| [240,480) | Scene: owner selected; VO [264,450), exact second sentence | Completion tick [450,474) follows speech |
| [480,720) | Scene: brand/CTA; VO [540,660), exact final sentence | A 1.5-second gap after the previous phrase introduces the name |
| [0,720) | Music with a restrained opening and final resolution | Continuity; duck under speech in finishing |

These are provisional reservations, not measured performance marks. Record an authorized scratch reading, mark actual words/breaths and ask Chris to approve timing with Motion. If "clear owner" needs a longer hold, revise the corresponding picture and cues together; do not silently shorten "clear" or truncate the last consonant. Canonical scenes/cues use stable distinct IDs and only the shared contract fields.

The source plan requests a client-owned newly recorded voice with paid-social release evidence, a client-owned music bed with synchronization/edit permission, and an original simple tick. Those rights are requested, not established. Chris resolves the recording/release and approved pronunciation of "Nest" before final production. A supplied finished voice file is a valid local source and avoids a model dependency. A cloud voice is an alternative only if Chris approves upload, provider, consent and budget.

## Review and hand-off

Direction acceptance: the name is clearly heard once, the completion tick conveys a settled action, music does not mask sentence starts, and the CTA gets an undistracted hold. Sound sends the final script/performance marks to Motion before the timing freeze. After approval, the record names the actual revision/fingerprint and exact source/take IDs.

The generation producer receives per-cue requirements and source rights. The finishing producer receives originals/hashes, gain/ducking intent and the saved preset. Delivery includes the measured stereo48k WAV, aligned cue stems, manifest, transcript/captions, cue sheet and source evidence. Motion checks the same fingerprint in the final render and remeasures the mux. A named listener still reviews words, sync, tone, mono and tails. All those production/listening results are **unverified** in this example.

Anti-pattern: "cinematic uplifting background and a professional voice" gives neither a usable entrance map nor a pronunciation/rights decision. The concrete treatment makes a disagreement reviewable without pretending that a prepared brief proves a good soundtrack.
