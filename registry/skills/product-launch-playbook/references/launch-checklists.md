# Launch checklists

Every item has an owner and a due date. An item with an owner of "the team" is not owned: put it on the lead's list.

## The T-minus checklist

| When | What must be true |
|---|---|
| T-14 days | Positioning approved, goals set, owners named, assets listed |
| T-7 | Copy and creative in review; landing page and tracking live in staging; the email scheduled |
| T-1 | Everything verified (links, forms, UTM, unsubscribe); support briefed; the incident plan shared |
| Launch day | The sequence of posts and sends with exact times; someone watching |
| T+1 and T+7 | Replies answered, numbers read, learnings recorded |

The tiers set how much of this applies: tier 1 uses all of it over 14 days, tier 2 runs 7 days, tier 3 is a changelog entry and one post over 1 day.

## A submission kit (community or directory launches)

- A tagline within the site's limit.
- A first comment written as the maker.
- A short, honest description.
- Answers to the three likeliest questions.
- No vote request, and no organised voting. Check the site's current rules before submitting, because they change.

## The incident plan, per launch-day dependency

For each dependency (the signup, the payment page, the API): what breaks, who notices, who decides, and the rollback or the holding message. Add the stop rule for scheduled posts if something serious happens.

## The measurement scheme

- One primary metric with a number (signups, trials started, qualified leads), one guardrail (support contacts, refund requests), a baseline from the last 28 days and a decision date.
- UTM: one `utm_campaign` for the launch, `utm_source` per channel, `utm_content` per asset.

## At T+7

Read the numbers against the goal and the guardrail. If they are below the goal, record why (message, channel or product) with evidence, and hand the next move to Ava as an experiment, not a second launch.
