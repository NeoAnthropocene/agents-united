## Context

Plan 033's shared foundation/catalog and approved author-skill loading/availability are merged through #199. The next accepted job is host-parametric realization before host-update sync. A native role alone cannot deliver it: the current Claude role planner requires a legacy agent source before selecting a native replacement, `nativeRoles` maps existing agents, and the factory binding checker is explicitly author-skill-specific. A permitted Subagent Contract identifier also does not establish loading, registration, execution or a checked result.

Prior art: [the concrete design and evidence inventory](../../plans/033-slice-4-claude-realize-design.md), the existing maintainer realize/sync skills, foundation and ADRs 0045/0046/0048/0050/0052, native package/projector/ownership tests and current Claude guides/profile/tool policy. ADRs 0051/0053–0055 and newer R4/completion/diagnostic observations constrain their scoped mechanisms; they do not turn the factory into an Agent Team or reopen R3. #206 already corrects the stale ADR 0054 status.

## Proposed decision

Use an **explicit factory-owned native binding** for the new Claude-only worker rather than adding an Antigravity-shaped legacy carrier solely to reach the native planner. Validate its shared pair and required native role, runbook, support/rule references and existing guard file/registration against the complete planned installed set before mutations. Track native-only outputs with bundle-only ownership, normal source hashes, update/remove and doctor behavior. Preserve the existing author-skill binding unchanged in meaning; opt-in absence leaves older bundles alone.

Review/implement the delivery prerequisite (P1) independently, using registry fixtures and full lifecycle proof, then the bounded worker (P2). The worker uses an unchanged four-field contract, a target-host job read by path+hash or supplied in full, a foreground ordinary subagent and a checked hand-back. Root and intent constraints are prose plus deterministic checks, not a new sandbox or first-action hook guarantee. Existing destructive guards keep their real failure limits.

Keep evaluator, main-thread factory orchestration, contract helper, Domain/Organization templates, reader distribution, sync and managed Option A installation as separately reviewed deliveries. The P2 worker may return a checked draft with quality review pending, but **slice 4a is incomplete** until the orchestrator checks its evaluator and the Claude stage has the required authorized evidence. Do not relabel intermediate delivery as a complete factory.

## Alternatives and consequences

A legacy carrier would reuse today's resolver but add a second source and fallback obligations for an unavailable host. Reusing engineering/code-review roles would mismatch their missions. Extending the built-in author-skill fork would conflate a one-skill authoring job with the planned host-parametric role and would not deliver the shared pair. A complete orchestrator/evaluator/helper/template change in one PR would prevent independent review of accepted requirements.

Native-only registration adds a real installer/ownership prerequisite, so it requires missing-edge, collision, package, direct-item, update/remove/doctor and full addon lifecycle evidence. It must not silently expand plugin/global/other-host support. Exact paths, public seams, red cases, fixed inputs, returned-result checks and unavailable prerequisites are in the linked design. No new hook, transport boundary, model run, host baseline change or legacy retirement is selected.

The new decisions for the maintainer are this registration strategy and the P1 → P2 worker-first boundary. Author-skill selection/loading, Option A and Claude → Codex → Antigravity → Cline remain accepted. Approval of this proposal would not authorize publication, merge or paid proof by itself.

## 2026-11-13 — accepted P8b stage 1 (manual import); stage 2 not accepted

The maintainer chose "Two stages (Recommended)" and then "Approve as proposed (Recommended)" for stage 1. Stage 1 is the manual-import route: the maintainer types `/author-skill <absolute task path>`, the `author-skill` fork writes its files, and the coordinator imports and checks them. Stage 2, the bare `Skill` grant to the coordinator, is a later and separate approval and is not accepted here.

Stage 1 adds:

- `registry/skills/coordinate-artifact/scripts/check-skill-return.mjs`, a caller-operated read-only checker (`--job <abs> [--result <abs>]`, `--help`) with a stable machine `code` on failure;
- two strict schemas, `assets/skill-task.schema.json` and `assets/skill-return.schema.json`;
- the runbook reference `references/skill-authoring.md` with the caller sequence;
- those four paths in the coordinator support closure (`COORDINATOR_SUPPORT` and the coordinator binding's `requiredSupport` go from 7 to 11 paths; `reviewedInputs` is untouched), with both coordinate-artifact entries and the native main's body text updated;
- one rule in the native `author-skill` entry: the single absolute path in the invocation text is the task record; read it and follow it. A neighbouring phrase changed from "in the invocation" to "in the task".

Stage 1 does not change any grant, the core definitions, the contract, the coordinator `tools` line, the worker closures, the installer, projector, launcher or CLI, or the P2 and P5 schemas and checkers. The coordinator still holds no Skill tool and does not start the fork.

Enforcement kinds, stated honestly. The checker is a deterministic script: it compares the final draft bytes with the baseline, the saved job and the return, and it always reports `quality: pending`, `executionProof: unverified`, `live: unverified` and `acceptedDraft: false`. A pass means consistency of bytes and records, not acceptance. Everything the fork is asked to do (read the task, run `draft.mjs` once, stay inside its roots) is prose. The checker cannot show who ran `draft.mjs`, that it ran exactly once, or that nothing outside the draft and producer roots was written. No per-Skill tool fence exists: the library documents `Skill(name)` forms for permission rules and similar settings, not for an agent file's `tools` field, and the installer refuses a deny overlay in the agent file.

Unverified, needing a maintainer-typed Host session: that the typed path reaches the fork as the invocation text; that the fork reads the task, runs `draft.mjs` once and writes only inside its roots; foreground behaviour; the fork's tool pool and whether the coordinator's hook applies to it; evaluator usefulness; repair adherence; the prepared with, without and control comparisons. The fork has no helper to compute the canonical `taskDigest` it must echo, so the runbook tells it to compute it, which may be awkward live. Linux and CI behaviour is unverified. No independent review of stage 1 has been done. Offline gates on the combined tree (host-library verify, typecheck, serial full suite, addon matrix) exited 0; the raw records are under `logs/plan-033-p8b-2026-11-13/`.

Stage 2 would need, before approval: primary evidence for the argument shape of the native Skill call (the library names no argument field; only `{"skill": "<name>"}` appears in recorded calls); a decision on the pointer directory; one maintainer-typed observation of a model-invoked skill with `$ARGUMENTS`; and acceptance that a bare `Skill` grant also reaches the factory's other forks and the dormant router entry, restricted by prose only. It would also need the `skill` capability in the core, `author-skill` in the coordinator contract (a reviewed relaxation of the binder's preload rule), the `author-skill` closure in the coordinator's checks, and a full addon matrix. None of this is authorised. This decision authorises no live Host, model or provider run, private-record access, paid evaluation, commit, push, PR publication or merge.
