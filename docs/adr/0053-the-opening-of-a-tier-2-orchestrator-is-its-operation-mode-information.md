# ADR 0053: The opening of a Tier-2 orchestrator is its Operation Mode information, and the mode-line gate waits for the saved transcript

- **Status**: Accepted, 2026-10-13. The maintainer, after Sitting U and the three red hook errors of its first minute, in their own words: "We should make a change in our tier-2 orchestrator agent welcome message rule, this cause more damage than the harm it brings to us. We should remove the welcoming message for digital-agency (and the other tier-2 bundle orchestrator agents too in the future); and change this to the first "Operation Mode" information for its first message. Test if this works without the hook errors as we expect, and than implement." Amends ADR 0051 (the lead's second message is the Operation Mode information and nothing else of its own; the introduction is gone) and ADR 0044 (decision 2, "No sleep or polling loop is added", no longer holds: the hook now waits for the saved transcript). The release rule of ADR 0044 is unchanged. **Verified in part in an interactive session** (Sitting V, 2026-10-13, G1 and a bare "Hi": no red hook error, and the opening was the line alone); the wait itself did not have to act in that sitting (see "Not established").
- **Context**:
  - **The welcome lived in four places.** The `<mandatory_first_turn_response>` of the canonical definition (`registry/agents/orchestrator-digital-agency.md`: the status block, then "Welcome! I'm Chris" and a roster of the team); the Claude lead's section "The first message" ("Under the line, introduce yourself as Chris and name your team", and the second message "then the introduction" of ADR 0051); the default kickoff of each launcher, which `agents start` sends when the user types no task ("Please introduce your coordinator role to the user and ask for their first task."); and the sentence of the Claude launcher that sends the lead to its first-message section "before the introduction".
  - **The welcome was not what produced the red errors, and it was a cost of its own.** In Sittings R to U the lead never followed the introduction rule in a session with a task, but on a bare "Hi" the current lead answers with the line, "I'm Chris", the roster of nine and a question. The red errors had two other causes. First, the lead keeps its line in its reasoning and makes its first gated call without a text block (0 of 5 interactive sessions followed the order of ADR 0051). Second, **the host saves its transcript late**, and the hook has no other source for the text of the message being written: the host's hooks guide says of `transcript_path` that it "may lag the in-memory conversation", and `PreToolUse` input carries no text. In Sitting U (H10m, session `cbb4adf8`) the lead did what the first hold said, wrote the line as a text block at +18.7 s and repeated the call, and was held at +20.2 s and again at +23.8 s ("The saved transcript has 0 text block(s) of yours", false: the line was in the lead's message); a call at +27.8 s was released. Across the interactive sessions of Sittings P, T and U the line was missing from the file 2.7, 5.0, 5.7 and 6.0 s after it was written, and present by 9 to 10 s. The second and third errors of Sitting U were false holds, caused by the lag alone.
  - **The trials of 2026-10-13** (headless, Sonnet at low effort, folders built from the clone, a task prompt that forces a gated call and a bare "Hi"; 29 runs; the records are in `host-library/claude/observations/2026-10-13-claude-2.1.296-first-message-trials.md`). `claude -p` skips the frontmatter hooks of a project agent (ADR 0042), so the gate was registered as a **settings-level hook** in the scratch folders, which `-p` trusts: the same script, the same input, and the gate exempts every call that carries an agent id. **The welcome was in the way.** The current lead was held twice in each of its two gated runs and wrote its line after the first hold; in none of its 3 task runs did the line come in an earlier message than its first gated call, and on "Hi" it answered with the line, "I'm Chris", the roster of nine and a question. With the welcome taken out and the order of ADR 0051 kept (variants A and E) the line came in an earlier message in 7 of 8 task runs (in the eighth it came with the call and was held). Taking out "introduction" alone was not enough on a greeting: variant A still said "Hi, I'm Chris, the lead for this digital agency team"; the paragraph that names what is left out ("I'm Chris", "your digital agency lead", the roster) and says what a bare hello gets is what produced "Hi. What would you like to work on?". **The line as the very first message**, written from the list of deferred tools that the session gives at its start (variants B, C and D, 15 runs), was written first in 15 of 15 and none of the 9 gated runs with the gate on was held, **but it disagreed with the lead's own searches in 4 of 15**: it put Firecrawl under Missing because the claude.ai connector (`mcp__claude_ai_Firecrawl__...`) was not yet in the list at the start (the lead corrected it in its next message each time), and in a fifth run the lead named Context7 without having searched for it. An accurate line needs the searches, so it comes after them, which is the order of ADR 0051. With that order, the No welcome paragraph and the waiting gate below (variant E, 5 runs) the lead wrote the line as a text block in its second message every time, in agreement with its own searches every time (in one the Firecrawl connector had failed with a 503, and the line said so), with no hold in the 4 gated runs. Headless runs cannot show the lag (the line, written 2 to 3 s before the call, was already in the file every time), so the lag is what an interactive session has to show.
- **Decision**:
  1. **The opening of a Tier-2 organization orchestrator is its Operation Mode information, and there is no welcome.** The first words to the user are the Tri-Tier mode and which required integrations are callable and missing, and nothing else of the lead's own: no greeting, no "welcome", no introduction of the lead ("I'm Chris", "your digital agency lead"), no roster, no list of what it can do unless the user asks. A bare "Hi" gets the information and one short question about what the user wants to work on. For digital-agency this changes the four places above: the `<mandatory_first_turn_response>` of the canonical definition (the status block stays, step 3 becomes the No welcome rule), the lead's section on Claude Code ("**No welcome.**", with the examples the lead was seen to ignore), the default kickoff of the three launchers ("Please ask the user for their first task.") and the Claude launcher's sentence ("before anything else"). The persona stays in the definition (the Identity and the roster of personas): nothing is lost but the greeting.
  2. **Every Tier-2 orchestrator with a definition of its own follows the rule, the ones that do not exist yet included.** `tests/orchestrator-opening.test.ts` reads `registry/bundles.json`: for each bundle of tier `organization` whose orchestrator is not shared with a domain bundle, the definition must carry the first-turn block with the status block and the No welcome rule, and no welcome text; the launchers and every native Claude orchestrator are checked too. The term is in `CONTEXT.md` ("Operation Mode information").
  3. **The order of ADR 0051 stays, minus the introduction.** The first message holds the searches; the second is the mode line, as a text block, and no other words of the lead's, with the one `ToolSearch` that loads the task tools; the first other call is in the third message. The line is written after the searches because it has to be accurate (the trials above).
  4. **The gate waits for the saved transcript before it holds a call.** When the line is not in the file, the hook looks at the file every 150 ms for up to 10 s (`AGENTS_UNITED_MODE_LINE_WAIT_MS`, `0` turns the wait off) and stops when it finds the line, which releases the call, or at the cap, which holds it with the message of its round. It waits in the first two rounds of holds only: a lead that never writes the line (Sitting S held two leads 11 and 13 times) does not pay the cap at each hold, and by the third round the file has had two rounds to catch up, so the count in that message is the file's. This lifts the line of ADR 0044, decision 2 ("No sleep or polling loop is added"), and the sentence of ADR 0051 that refused a wait. Both refused **elapsed time as a release**, and that is kept: **only the line in the saved transcript releases a call**, and the end of the wait releases nothing. What is new is that the hook waits for evidence that is on its way instead of holding a call, and so showing an error, because the host's writer is late. The round of a hold is counted when the hook holds, and the state is read again after the wait, so that the calls of one message, which wait together, are still one round. An unreadable transcript, a missing session id, a bad payload, a teammate's call and `ToolSearch` still pass at once, and anything that goes wrong inside the gate lets the call through. Each decision is one line of a log beside the marker (`%TEMP%\agents-united\mode-line-<session>.log`: the time, the tool, `waiting`, `released` or `held`, the round, the number of text blocks the file held, how long the hook waited, and why); it holds no text and no tool input, so that the next sitting can say how long the save took.
- **Consequences**:
  - A lead that follows the order shows no red error, even when the host's save is late, provided the save arrives within ten seconds; a lead that does not write the line is held as before, after waiting up to ten seconds at each of its first two holds for a line it did not write (the hook cannot tell a line in flight from a line never written), and from the third hold at once, with the count that the file then really holds. The hold messages, the rounds and the stop rules of ADR 0051 are unchanged and remain the fallback.
  - The wait costs a second or two in a session whose line is late, and up to ten seconds at each of the first two holds of a session whose lead did not write the line. If the host's writer turns out not to flush while a hook is running (the evidence of Sittings T and U does not rule it out: two hooks of one message gave different answers 0.1 s apart, which suggests it does flush), the wait would cost its cap at every held call and gain nothing; the log shows it (`held` with `by: cap` and no `released` after a wait), and `AGENTS_UNITED_MODE_LINE_WAIT_MS=0` returns to the behaviour of ADR 0051.
  - The launchers no longer ask a lead to introduce itself on a start without a task; a user who types a task is unaffected.
  - The canonical definition's roster blurb is gone from the first turn on every host; the delegation-first policy it repeated is in the definition's own section ("Subagent-First Delegation Policy"), so no instruction is lost.
- **Not established**: that the host flushes the transcript while the hook polls, and how long its save takes: in Sitting V the log of G1 shows one release at the first look (`released`, `by: line`, 1 ms), because the line was already in the file 4.3 s after it was written, so no slow save was there to test; a session in which the save is late will show `waiting` and then `released` with `by: line-after-wait` and the milliseconds, or `held` with `by: cap`; whether an interactive lead follows the order and the No welcome rule as the headless trials did (4 of 4, and a greeting answered with one question): Sitting V is 1 of 1 for G1 and the greeting was answered as asked, against 0 of 5 interactive sessions before the text changed; the full-roster regressions R2 and R3; whether the engineering lead and the other domain orchestrators should open the same way (the rule here is for Tier-2 organization orchestrators, as asked).
